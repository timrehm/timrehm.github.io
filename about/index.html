My name is Tim Rehm and I grew up in Connecticut. I am a physicst, engineer, instrument scientist, and data analyst. Did college at Cornell University and received my PhD in Physics from Brown University in May 2026. My thesis was on the development and deployment of the EXCITE mission. I designed and assembled the cryogenic system, detrended systematics from measured timestream detector signals, produced radiometric models of the instrument to quantify photon signal and noise levels, constructed pointing jitter simulations, and provided mission support of the payload during a 2024 test flight from Fort Sumner, New Mexico. Overall, it was a great time.

And it continues to be a great time! Because I now work at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center as a NASA Postdoctoral Program Fellow. I perform many similar tasks as I did in graduate school. We are launching EXCITE from McMurdo Station in Antarctica during Winter 2026-27, and I will need to be there to prepare the payload for launch and ultimately operate it during its flight.

Above all else, I love baseball and I really love the Boston Red Sox. Here's me housing a hot dog during Game 3 of the 2021 ALCS. As a child to baseball-apathetic, Yankee-leaning parents born in New York, the first crossroads of my life came on the first day of kindergarten, when the first friend I had ever made invited me into his enigmatic, diehard Red Sox family. He was in the midst of setting a world record for the most consecutive days wearing a Red Sox T-shirt or jersey, a record enthusiastically encouraged by his mother and grandfather, the latter of whom was a wooden toymaker and deacon who would end every blessing with, "and may God let the Red Sox win today." I was hooked and never looked back. The rest is already told: 2004, 2007, 2013, and 2018. I played my share of baseball, too, while the Red Sox were stockpiling Commissioner's Trophies. I started playing competitive ball at age 10, played through high school, and even got the chance to get behind the dish for the Brown club team, as shown in the photo. Baseball is always a part of my diurnal cycle, and I hope to use this page to share any statistical findings I come across while I peruse the bottomless reserve of baseball statistics.
